Delegate (Permission Delegation)

Delegate lets you grant specific, granular permissions to another XRPL account without giving them your private keys. Useful for shared control, delegated operations (e.g., signing certain tx types, managing sub-accounts), or institutional workflows. Revoke or update permissions at any time with another DelegateSet transaction.

🔐 Grant / Update Permissions DelegateSet
Delegate ✅ Required (target account address)
Permissions ✅ Required (array of permission flags/strings)
Max 10 permissions per delegate Limit per entry
Flags / Metadata ➕ Optional (description, expiration, etc.)
🚫 Revoke / Remove Delegate DelegateSet (clear)
Delegate ✅ Required (address to revoke)
Permissions ❌ Empty array or omit to remove all
Signer ✅ Must be the delegating account (owner)
Important Notes:
  • Requires the **Delegate** amendment (or related permission delegation amendment) to be enabled on the network.
  • Each delegate entry creates a **Delegate** ledger object owned by your account, consuming **owner reserve** (~0.2 XRP per delegate) until removed.
  • Permissions is an array of specific rights (e.g., "Payment", "TrustSet", "OfferCreate", "EscrowFinish", custom strings) — only listed permissions are granted.
  • Delegates cannot sign for you in general (unlike regular key or multi-sign) — they can only perform the explicitly allowed actions on your behalf.
  • Only the delegating account (owner) can add, update, or remove delegates — delegates cannot delegate further unless explicitly permitted.
  • Changes take effect immediately upon ledger inclusion; no approval needed from the delegate account.
  • Use cases: allow a hot wallet to pay bills, let a custodian manage offers, grant limited access to a service without full control.

Security & Revocation Risks

Delegating permissions gives real control — a compromised delegate can misuse allowed actions until revoked.

  • Once granted, permissions are **active immediately** — if the delegate account is hacked, they can perform allowed txs until you submit a revocation DelegateSet.
  • Revocation is **not retroactive** — any transactions the delegate already signed and submitted before revocation are irreversible.
  • Keep delegate lists small and trusted — too many delegates increase reserve cost and attack surface.
  • Reserve remains locked per delegate entry — forgetting to revoke unused delegates keeps paying owner reserve indefinitely.
  • Delegate permissions are **public on the ledger** — anyone can see who has what access to your account.
  • Always test delegation flows (grant → use → revoke) on devnet or Testnet first — incorrect permission strings or formats cause tx failure and fee burn.

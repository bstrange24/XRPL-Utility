Credentials (Attestations & Verifications)

Credentials allow issuers to create verifiable attestations (e.g., KYC status, membership, age proof) that subjects can accept and present. Useful for compliance, permissioned access (DepositAuth, Permissioned Domains), and on-chain reputation without sharing personal data beyond the credential ID.

🪪 Issue Credential CredentialCreate
Subject ✅ Required (destination address)
CredentialType ✅ Required (unique string identifier)
Data / URI ➕ Optional (on-chain data or pointer)
Flags (e.g. tfAccept) ➕ Optional (auto-accept if set)
✅ Accept / Delete Credential Subject or Issuer Actions
CredentialAccept ➕ Subject accepts (shifts reserve to subject)
CredentialDelete ➕ Issuer deletes (if not accepted) or after revocation
Credential ID ✅ Required for accept/delete
📝 Important Notes:
  • Requires the Credentials amendment to be enabled on the network.
  • Issuer pays initial owner reserve (~0.2 XRP) when creating; reserve shifts to the subject upon acceptance (via CredentialAccept).
  • CredentialType is a unique string per issuer (e.g., "KYC-2025", "MemberLevel1") — combine with subject to form unique credential ID.
  • Data/URI can be on-chain (small JSON) or point off-chain — contents are public once issued.
  • Accepted credentials can be used for permissioned features (e.g., DepositPreauth, Permissioned Domains, future XLS proposals).
  • Issuer can delete unaccepted credentials; once accepted, deletion typically requires subject cooperation or revocation logic (if implemented via amendment rules).
  • Credentials are immutable once accepted — issuers cannot change data after subject acceptance.

⚠️ Irreversibility & Privacy Considerations

Credentials are public on the ledger and carry compliance responsibilities — issue carefully.

  • Once a subject accepts a credential, the data becomes permanently associated with their address — no native way to retract or edit without deletion (if allowed).
  • Issuing false/inaccurate attestations can have legal/compliance consequences — ensure accuracy and obtain consent where required.
  • Reserve lockup moves to the subject upon acceptance — if subject never accepts, issuer remains responsible for the reserve cost until deleted.
  • Deletion of accepted credentials may be restricted or require specific conditions (check current amendment rules) — plan for revocation strategies if needed.
  • Test thoroughly on devnet/Testnet — wrong credential types or data formats cause transaction failure and fee burn.
  • Credentials expose issuer-subject relationships publicly — consider privacy implications for sensitive attestations.
